# Worked Examples

Three complete plans, written the way the course teaches. Use them as models for your own. The names and figures are illustrative.

---

## Example 1 — Luxury travel: building the Q1 pipeline

**Life Map area:** Commercial Leadership
**Role:** The Trusted Advisor
**Horizon:** Six weeks (mid-November to end of December)

### Result

Twelve qualified luxury travel advisors in our three priority markets have had a substantive first conversation with us, and at least four have a sailing or stay under discussion, by 31 December.

### Reason

- So January opens with a pipeline already in motion, instead of being spent building one.
- Because the advisors who matter most plan their clients' spring and summer travel in December. If we're not in that conversation, someone else is.
- So the team sees what a deliberate quarter looks like, and starts to work that way themselves.
- Because I'd rather be known by twelve advisors for being genuinely useful than by two hundred for sending newsletters.

### Route

| Action | Must | Time | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Define "qualified" — client profile, booking volume, markets | ✓ | 45 min | Me | Week 1, Mon |
| Build a shortlist of 30 advisors against that definition | ✓ | 2 hrs | Sales coordinator, I review | Week 1 |
| Write one short, personal opening note — no brochure, one relevant idea for their clients | ✓ | 60 min | Me | Week 1, Wed |
| Send 10 notes a week, personalised | ✓ | 90 min/wk | Me | Weeks 2–4, Tue |
| Hold first conversations: 30 min, their clients first, our product second | ✓ | 6 hrs total | Me | Weeks 2–6 |
| Same-day follow-up with one specific, useful thing | ✓ | 15 min each | Me, coordinator drafts | After each call |
| Weekly pipeline review — 20 minutes, Friday | | 20 min/wk | Me + coordinator | Fridays |
| Redesign the trade presentation | | — | — | Q1 |
| Attend the year-end trade mixer | | 3 hrs | Optional | If two priority advisors attend |

**Leverage notes:** The coordinator builds the shortlist and drafts follow-ups from my call notes. I keep the opening notes and the conversations — those are only-me work, because the relationship is the product.

## Example 2 — Specialty coffee: a calmer, more profitable December

**Life Map area:** The Coffee House
**Role:** The Host
**Horizon:** Four weeks

### Result

Through December, the shop runs every peak shift without me on the bar, average ticket is up 8% on last December, and no regular waits more than six minutes at the busiest hour.

### Reason

- Because the holiday season is when new guests decide whether we're their place. The experience has to hold under pressure.
- So I can spend December where I'm most useful — on the floor with guests, with suppliers, and on January's plan — rather than pulling shots.
- So the team proves to themselves they can run a full rush. That confidence lasts longer than the season.
- Because a calm December means a rested January, and I'd like to start the year with energy.

### Route

| Action | Must | Time | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Map the 7:30–9:30 rush: where do orders slow down? | ✓ | 2 mornings observing | Me | Week 1 |
| Rework bar positions and the milk-steaming workflow from what I see | ✓ | 60 min | Me + head barista | Week 1 |
| Write a one-page peak-shift standard: roles, timings, what "good" looks like | ✓ | 90 min | Me | Week 1 |
| Train each barista on it, one shift each | ✓ | 4 shifts | Head barista | Week 2 |
| Introduce two seasonal pairings at the till (drink + pastry) with a short script | ✓ | 45 min | Me | Week 2 |
| Weekly 15-minute numbers check: ticket, wait times, waste | ✓ | 15 min/wk | Me | Mondays |
| Automate the weekly milk and pastry order from par levels | | 60 min | Me, once | Week 1 |
| Redesign the loyalty card | | — | — | February |

**Leverage notes:** The head barista owns training. The supplier order becomes a par-level system instead of a weekly judgement call. I keep the guest experience standard and the numbers.

## Example 3 — Personal: getting strength back

**Life Map area:** Strength & Stamina
**Role:** The Athlete (in progress)
**Horizon:** Eight weeks

### Result

I complete 28 training sessions in eight weeks — at least three a week, never two weeks below three — and can do a 5 km run without stopping by the end of January.

### Reason

- Because I'm noticeably more patient, sharper and kinder on days I've trained.
- So the busiest commercial season of the year doesn't cost me my health, as it did last year.
- Because the people I care about deserve the version of me with energy at 8pm, not the one who's done by 5.
- Because I want to keep promises to myself as reliably as I keep them to clients.

### Route

| Action | Must | Time | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book four 45-minute sessions in the calendar as recurring blocks | ✓ | 10 min | Me | Today |
| Lay out kit the night before | ✓ | 2 min | Me | Every evening before |
| Follow a simple programme — two strength, two runs | ✓ | — | Me (or a coach) | Ongoing |
| Track sessions on one card on the fridge | ✓ | 10 sec | Me | After each session |
| Sleep: phone out of the bedroom by 22:30 | ✓ | — | Me | Nightly |
| Find a training partner for one session a week | | 20 min | Me | Week 2 |
| Rebuild the diet entirely | | — | — | Not this quarter |

**Leverage notes:** The leverage here is environmental — kit ready, sessions pre-booked, the card on the fridge. It's not about more willpower. It's about fewer decisions.

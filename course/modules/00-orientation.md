# Module 0 — Orientation

*Read time: 10 minutes. Exercise: 20 minutes.*

---

## How this course works

Each module has three parts:

1. **The idea** — a short argument for why something matters.
2. **The method** — how to do it, step by step.
3. **The practice** — a written exercise, and something to try over the following week.

The writing is the course. Reading without doing the exercises will give you a pleasant sense of agreement and very little change. The exercises are short on purpose. Do them in one sitting, by hand or in a single document you keep for the whole course.

## The pace

One module per week is the right rhythm for most people. It gives each idea a full week to be tested against real life — a busy Monday, a disappointing Thursday, a weekend that got away.

If you prefer to move faster, do Modules 1–3 in the first week (they are mostly thinking), then slow down. Modules 4–7 need repetition to work.

## What you'll need

- A notebook, or one digital document you'll keep for the whole course
- Your calendar for the last two weeks and the next two
- 60 minutes, once a week, that you protect as you would a client meeting

That last one matters more than the other two combined.

## Exercise 0.1 — Your baseline

Answer quickly. First instincts are more useful than considered ones here.

**1. Rate each statement from 1 (rarely true) to 5 (almost always true).**

| Statement | Score |
|---|---|
| At the start of most days, I know the three things that matter most. | |
| At the end of most weeks, I feel I moved something important forward. | |
| My calendar reflects what I say my priorities are. | |
| I spend most of my working hours on work only I can do. | |
| I have regular time for the people and things that restore me. | |
| When my plans fall apart, I can rebuild them quickly. | |
| I know why my current main goals matter to me personally. | |
| **Total (out of 35)** | |

Keep this number. You'll score yourself again at the end of Module 7.

**2. Finish these sentences.**

- The part of my life that gets the least intentional attention is…
- The thing I keep saying I'll get to is…
- I'm most at my best when…
- If the next 90 days went well, the clearest sign would be…

**3. Name your one reason for taking this course.**

Not the general one ("to be more productive"). The specific one. *"So I stop answering supplier messages at dinner."* *"So the Q1 pipeline gets built before February, not during it."* Write it at the top of your course notebook.

## Before Module 1

For the next three days, notice — without changing anything — the moments you feel busy but not useful. Just write a word or two each time: *inbox*, *chasing invoice*, *meeting that could have been a message*. You'll use these notes in Module 2.

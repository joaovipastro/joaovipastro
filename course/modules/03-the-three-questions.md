# Module 3 — The Three Questions

*Read time: 20 minutes. Exercise: 40 minutes.*

> **The idea in one line:** Before you decide what to do, decide what you want and why. The action plan becomes shorter, clearer and easier to follow.

---

## The idea

Everything in this course rests on three questions:

1. **Result** — What do I actually want?
2. **Reason** — Why does it matter to me?
3. **Route** — What's the simplest path there?

They're simple enough to remember under pressure, which is the point. You can ask them before a week, a meeting, a difficult conversation or a two-hour block on a Thursday afternoon.

What follows is how to answer each one well. Weak answers produce weak plans. Most of the skill in this method is in the writing.

---

## Question 1: Result — What do I actually want?

A Result is an **outcome**, not an activity. It describes what will be true when you're done, not what you'll be doing along the way.

| Activity (weak) | Result (strong) |
|---|---|
| Work on the Q1 pipeline | Twelve qualified luxury travel advisors have had a first conversation with us by 31 January |
| Fix the menu | The winter menu is finalised, priced at a 70% gross margin, and printed by 15 November |
| Exercise more | I complete four training sessions this week |
| Talk to the team | Every barista knows the new opening checklist and has done it once under supervision |

### Five tests of a well-written Result

1. **Is it an outcome?** Could someone see it and confirm it's done?
2. **Is it specific?** Numbers, names, dates where they help.
3. **Is it yours?** Is it within your influence, or does it depend entirely on someone else saying yes? (*"Sign the resort group account"* depends on them. *"Present a proposal their commercial director calls the strongest they've seen this year"* is closer to yours.)
4. **Is it the right size?** A weekly Result should be achievable in a week. A quarterly one in a quarter.
5. **Does it pull you?** Read it aloud. If it sounds like a chore, it's either the wrong Result or it needs a better Reason.

### Find the result behind the task

When a task appears, ask: *"What result is this in service of?"* Keep asking until you reach something that matters.

> Task: Update the CRM.
> → So that? I can see which accounts are going cold.
> → So that? I can re-engage them before Q1 budgets are set.
> → **Result:** Ten dormant accounts re-engaged with a personal note before 15 December.

Sometimes the chain leads nowhere. That's a task you can stop doing.

## Question 2: Reason — Why does it matter to me?

The Reason is the fuel. It's what gets you to make the call when you'd rather not, to stay focused at 4pm, to keep going on day nine when the novelty has gone.

People skip this question because it feels soft. It's the most practical of the three. A Result without a Reason is a line on a list. A Result with a strong Reason is something you actually want to do.

### What makes a Reason strong

- **It's personal.** Not *"because the business needs it"* but *"because I want to stop worrying about January every December."*
- **It's emotional.** Logic tells you what to do. Emotion decides whether you do it. A good Reason makes you feel something when you read it.
- **It has more than one layer.** Write three or four reasons. What it will give you. What it will give the people you care about. What it will spare you. What it says about who you are.

### An example

> **Result:** The winter menu is finalised, priced at a 70% gross margin, and printed by 15 November.
>
> **Reason:**
> - So I walk into the busiest season with a menu I'm proud of, rather than patching it in December.
> - So the team has three weeks to learn it properly before the rush — they deserve to feel prepared.
> - So the margin pays for the second grinder in January without touching savings.
> - Because the menu is the first thing a new guest reads. It should say exactly who we are.

That's a Result you'll get done.

### "Have to" into "get to"

A useful test: try saying *"I get to…"* in front of your Result. *"I get to build the menu for our busiest season."* If it sounds false, look for a better Reason, or ask whether this Result is truly yours.

## Question 3: Route — What's the simplest path there?

Only now do you think about actions. And because you know exactly what you want and why, the list is far shorter than it would otherwise have been.

### How to write a Route

1. **Brainstorm** every action that could contribute to the Result. Don't filter.
2. **Circle the essential few.** Usually three to five actions will produce most of the Result. Those are your **musts**.
3. **Sequence** them. What has to happen first?
4. **Estimate** time for each. Be honest; then add a quarter.
5. **Assign** anything that doesn't need to be you. (Module 5 goes deeper on this.)
6. **Schedule** the musts. An action without a time is a hope.

### Example Route

> **Result:** Winter menu finalised, priced at 70% GM, printed by 15 November.
>
> | Action | Must? | Time | Who | When |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Shortlist 6 drinks and 4 food items from this year's tastings | ✓ | 60 min | Me | Mon 9:00 |
> | Get updated green-coffee and dairy prices | ✓ | 20 min | Ana | Mon |
> | Cost each item and test pricing | ✓ | 90 min | Me | Tue 9:00 |
> | Final tasting with two senior baristas | ✓ | 45 min | Me + team | Wed 15:00 |
> | Brief designer and approve proof | ✓ | 30 min | Me | Thu |
> | Research seasonal menus from other cafés | | 60 min | — | Optional |
> | Redesign the menu layout | | — | — | Next season |
>
> Five musts, about four hours of my time. The rest can wait or go.

### The rule of the essential few

You will always be able to think of more actions. The skill is in seeing which few will produce most of the result, and giving those your best hours. Everything else is optional until the musts are done.

## Exercise 3.1 — Three plans

Choose three Results for the next 7–14 days:

- One from your **lead area** (identified in Module 1)
- One from **work or business**
- One from **health, relationships or something personal**

For each, write the full plan on a separate page:

```
RESULT
(One sentence. Outcome, specific, yours, right-sized.)

REASON
- 
- 
- 
- 

ROUTE
Action | Must? | Time | Who | When
```

Then run each Result through the five tests above. Rewrite anything that fails.

## Exercise 3.2 — The task translation

Take ten items from your current to-do list. For each, ask *"What result is this in service of?"* until you reach something meaningful — or nothing.

Sort them into three groups:

- **Belongs to a Result** → move it into that Route
- **Maintenance** → move it to a separate maintenance list (Module 4 explains how to handle these)
- **Leads nowhere** → delete it

---

## This week's practice

Before any meeting or block of work longer than 30 minutes, write the three answers in the margin. One line each. *Result. Reason. Route.* It takes less than a minute and you'll notice the difference within two days.

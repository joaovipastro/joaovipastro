# Module 6 — Time, Focus and State

*Read time: 20 minutes. Exercise: 40 minutes.*

> **The idea in one line:** Your calendar shows what you actually prioritise. Your state decides how well you execute. Design both on purpose.

---

## The idea

By now you have a Life Map, a way of writing plans, a weekly ritual and a Leverage Ledger. This module is about the two things that decide whether any of it works on an ordinary Tuesday: **where your time goes** and **what state you're in when you spend it**.

---

## Part 1: Time targets

Ask most people what matters to them and you'll hear: family, health, building something meaningful. Look at their calendars and you'll see: email, meetings, commuting, more email.

This isn't hypocrisy. It's what happens when the calendar is filled by whoever asks first. The important things don't make noise. They don't send reminders. They wait, politely, until there's time — and there's never time.

**Time targets** reverse this. Instead of waiting to see what's left, you decide in advance how much of your week each area of your life deserves, and you book it first.

### How to set time targets

1. **List your Life Map areas.**
2. **For each, decide the minimum weekly time** that would keep it healthy, and the ideal.
3. **Total the minimums.** They must fit inside your real week, with room for the unexpected. If they don't, adjust now.
4. **Block the minimums** in your calendar as recurring appointments, before anything else goes in.

| Area | Minimum / week | Ideal / week | Where in the week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength & Stamina | 4 × 45 min | 5 × 60 min | Mon, Tue, Thu, Sat 7:00 |
| The People Who Matter | 2 dinners + Sunday | 3 dinners + Sunday | Tue, Fri evening; Sunday |
| Commercial Leadership | 3 × 90 min must time | 5 × 90 min | Mon–Fri 9:00–10:30 |
| The Coffee House | 6 hrs on-site + 1 hr review | 8 hrs + 2 hrs | Wed, Sat; Mon review |
| The Craft | 2 hrs | 4 hrs | Sunday morning |

### Protect your best hours

Urgency is a thief. Whatever arrives with the most noise will take your best hours unless you've already given them to something.

- Put must time **before** the inbox, not after.
- Check messages at set times — for example 10:30, 13:30 and 16:30 — rather than continuously.
- Turn off notifications during must time. Tell the people who need to know.
- In a role that genuinely requires responsiveness (a host on shift, a salesperson during peak booking season), protect a smaller block — even 45 minutes — and guard it closely.

### A note on the unpredictable week

Hospitality, travel and retail are not predictable. That's exactly why time targets help. When a day collapses, you know what the minimum is for each area, and you can move a block rather than lose it.

## Part 2: Focus

Focus isn't a personality trait. It's a set of conditions you can arrange.

- **One Result per block.** Before a block starts, write what result it's for. When your attention wanders, come back to that line.
- **Group similar work.** Calls with calls, writing with writing, admin with admin. Switching between types of work is expensive.
- **Clear the surface.** Close everything not needed for this Result. Phone face down, in another room if possible.
- **Finish on a visible step.** Stop each block by writing the very next action. Starting again becomes easy.

## Part 3: State

Two people with the same plan and the same calendar will get very different results depending on the state they bring to it. Tired, anxious and scattered, a simple task drags on all afternoon. Clear, rested and engaged, a difficult one can be done before lunch.

You can't control every circumstance. You can learn which small things reliably bring you back to your best state — and use them on purpose, rather than waiting to feel better.

### Your Restoratives Menu

A Restoratives Menu is a short, personal list of things that reliably lift your state. Like a restaurant menu, it's organised by size, so there's always something that fits the moment.

| Size | Time | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Small plates** | 1–5 minutes | Ten slow breaths. Step outside. A glass of water. One song, loud. A message to someone you love. Standing at the window with an espresso, no phone. |
| **Mains** | 15–60 minutes | A walk without headphones. A training session. Cooking. Reading something unrelated to work. A call with a friend. |
| **Tasting menus** | Half a day or more | A morning in nature. A long lunch with no agenda. A day trip. A tasting at a roaster you admire. |

### How to build yours

1. Think back to moments in the last year when you felt most like yourself. What were you doing?
2. List at least 15 items across the three sizes. Be specific. *"Walking"* is vague; *"walking along the water at 7am"* is something you can actually do.
3. Note which items cost money, need other people, or need planning. Make sure plenty of small plates need none of those.
4. Put the menu somewhere you'll see it — phone wallpaper, the inside of your notebook, the office wall.

### When to use it

- **Before must time**, a small plate to arrive properly
- **Between demanding blocks**, a small plate to reset
- **When you notice you're stuck, irritable or anxious**, a small plate before you do anything else
- **Each week**, at least one main, booked in advance
- **Each month**, at least one tasting menu

The point isn't indulgence. Your state is a working asset. Looking after it is part of the job.

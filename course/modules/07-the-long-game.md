# Module 7 — The Long Game

*Read time: 20 minutes. Exercise: 60 minutes.*

> **The idea in one line:** A system is only as good as the rituals that keep it alive. Build a few, keep them simple, and use them for years.

---

## The idea

Most planning systems die quietly around week six. Not because they stop working, but because life gets busy, a few sessions are skipped, and the habit never quite comes back.

This module is about making the method last. It covers three things:

1. **Daily rituals** that keep you anchored
2. **Reviews** at different time scales, so you stay on course
3. **Resets** for when you fall off — because you will

It also asks a bigger question: *whose game are you playing?*

---

## Design your own game

Many people live by rules they never chose. Success means a particular title, a particular income, a particular kind of busy. They win games they didn't want to play, and wonder why winning feels flat.

A better approach is to write your own rules. What does a good year look like *to you*? What would make you feel you'd won — not to anyone else, but to yourself?

### Exercise 7.1 — Your rules for winning

Write five to seven short rules that define a good life by your own measure. Make them things you can achieve regularly, not once in a lifetime.

Examples:

- *I win when I've trained four times this week.*
- *I win when a guest leaves happier than they arrived, and I had something to do with it.*
- *I win when I've had a real conversation with each of my closest people this week.*
- *I win when I end the working day at a time I chose.*
- *I win when I've learned something about coffee I didn't know on Monday.*

Notice that none of these depend on circumstances outside your control. That's deliberate. If your rules for winning are all about outcomes you can't guarantee, you'll spend most of your life losing. Rules you can meet regularly create momentum, and momentum is what produces the big outcomes in the end.

## Daily rituals

Keep these short. A ritual you can do on your worst day is better than an ideal one you skip.

### Morning (10–15 minutes)

1. **Small plate** from your Restoratives Menu — before the phone
2. **Read your Life Map** — 90 seconds
3. **Gratitude** — three specific things, written down
4. **Daily Card** — three Results, their musts, your protected block

### Evening (5 minutes)

1. **Close the day** — tick what's done, move what isn't
2. **One win** — write down one thing that went well, however small
3. **Tomorrow's first action** — so the morning starts with a decision already made

## The review rhythm

| Review | When | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily Card** | Each morning | 10 min | Decide today |
| **Weekly Mise en Place** | Same time each week | 60 min | Capture, group, plan, commit |
| **Monthly look back** | First weekend of the month | 30 min | Spot patterns; adjust time targets |
| **Quarterly Review** | Every 13 weeks | 2–3 hrs | Update the Life Map; set quarterly Results |
| **Annual vision** | Once a year | Half a day | Rewrite visions; decide what the next year is for |

### The monthly look back (30 minutes)

Read your last four weekly plans. Ask:

1. Which Results did I achieve? Which kept rolling over?
2. Which areas got their time targets? Which didn't?
3. What's one thing I'll do differently next month?

### The Quarterly Review (2–3 hours)

This is where the whole system is recalibrated. Use the [Quarterly Review template](../tools/quarterly-review.md). In outline:

1. **Celebrate** — list everything that went well this quarter. Be generous.
2. **Learn** — what didn't go to plan, and what did it teach you?
3. **Re-score** your Life Map. Update the current standard for each area.
4. **Refine** your visions. Have any changed?
5. **Choose quarterly Results** — one or two per area, three at most for your lead area.
6. **Write a Reason and a high-level Route** for each.
7. **Update** your time targets, Leverage Ledger and Restoratives Menu.
8. **Book** your weekly sessions and next Quarterly Review.

## Resets: when you fall off

You will miss a weekly session. Then another. A busy season, a trip, an illness, a launch. Suddenly it's been a month and the system feels like something you used to do.

This is normal. The method doesn't depend on unbroken discipline. It depends on knowing how to come back.

### The 20-minute reset

When you notice you've drifted:

1. **Capture** — ten minutes, everything on your mind
2. **Pick three** — the three Results that matter most this week
3. **One must** — the single most important action for tomorrow, in the calendar

That's it. Don't try to catch up on everything you missed. Next week, do a full Weekly Mise en Place as normal.

### Avoid the restart trap

Many people treat a lapse as a reason to start over with a new system, a new app, a new notebook. Don't. The method hasn't failed. You just paused. Pick it up where you left it.

Open your calendar and book:

- Monthly look back, first weekend of each of the next three months
- Your first Quarterly Review, 13 weeks from today

## Exercise 7.3 — Refine your Life Map

Return to the Life Map you drafted in Module 1. With six weeks of practice behind you, rewrite it. Sharpen the visions. Update the current standards. Check the roles still fit.

## Exercise 7.4 — Your baseline, again

Return to the baseline questions in [Module 0](00-orientation.md) and score yourself again. Compare the two totals. Then answer:

1. What changed most?
2. Which part of the method made the biggest difference?
3. Which part will you need to work hardest to keep?

## Closing

The method is simple on purpose. Three questions. A weekly hour. A daily card. A few rituals and reviews. None of it is complicated; all of it is easy to skip.

What makes it work is the same thing that makes a great hotel or a great café work: consistency in the details, done with care, day after day, until it becomes simply how things are done.

Set your station. Then cook.
